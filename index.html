One slow link can stall the entire ring

CHARM+ reorganizes sequential communications into levels to enable intra-level parallelism and computation overlap, and decouples cross- from intra-hardware communication to eliminate bandwidth-imbalance-induced synchronization overhead.

01 / The bottleneck

Ring-Attention: Serialization and Bandwidth-Imbalance Sensitivity

Sixteen GPUs form a single ring across two nodes. Each round transfers KV to the next rank. Intra-node links have variable arrival times, while the two cross-node boundaries are slower.

Illustrative bandwidth ratios, not measured hardware bandwidth. Intra-node links vary independently; cross-node links are slower. The synchronized ring round waits for the slowest transfers.

Hierarchical communication

Intra-node staggered three-phase KV/Q rotation and bidirectional asynchronous cross-node diameter Q exchange replace a single fixed neighbor ring.

Multistream overlap

Cross-node Q is decoupled from intra-node work, so local KV/Q transfers are never blocked by slower cross-node traffic.

Second pass + return

After cross-node Q arrives and the first three phases finish, the same pattern runs with cross-node Q in the Q slots, then results are returned in a final bidirectional pass.

02 / The approach

CHARM+ does three things

Shorten communication dependency chains, overlap independent work, and isolate slow cross-node traffic.

02a / CHARM+ communication schedule

Three-phase intra-node KV/Q transfer · bidirectional asynchronous cross-node Q

Same 16 GPUs on the same circle. Cross-node Q moves asynchronously and bidirectionally between diameter counterparts (0↔8, 1↔9, …): each side sends its own Q while receiving the partner's Q, without blocking intra-node transfers. Intra-node KV/Q transfers run in three phases: phase 1 pairs i↔i^1, phase 2 works inside each 4-GPU group, phase 3 crosses the two 4-GPU groups. KV is one-way; Q is round-trip. Intra-node transfers are drawn as arcs outside the circle and disappear when the transfer ends. After the first round and cross-node Q complete, a second three-phase round runs with cross-node-received Q in the Q slots; after that, one more bidirectional cross-node Q pass returns the results.

Intra-node communication uses four fixed concentric visual lanes: the two inner lanes are reserved for KV, and the two outer lanes for Q (cross-node Q received in Round 2). Concurrent transfers of the same type use separate sub-lanes, so KV and Q do not appear to share a path. Each arc appears only while its transfer is active, then fades out. Cross-node Q uses solid links and travels in both directions simultaneously. Links are hidden behind the central panel and disappear with their particles when the transfer completes. Cross-node Q overlaps with intra-node communication and does not block the three intra-node phases. After all three phases in Round 1 and the cross-node Q transfers complete, Round 2 runs the same three-phase pattern. KV remains one-way, while the Q slots carry cross-node-received Q (purple, round-trip). After Round 2, one final bidirectional cross-node Q pass returns the results to the partner GPUs.
